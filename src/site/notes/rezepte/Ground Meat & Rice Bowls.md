# Ground Meat & Rice Bowls

*One system, three levels — from freezer-only to better than takeout*

![Three bowls of ground meat over rice: burger beef with pickles, gochujang chicken with spring onion, cumin lamb with yellow rice and salad|500](/img/user/images/ground-meat-and-rice-bowls.png)

Every bowl is browned ground meat over rice. Each level adds one layer of components; swap any component for another in its category and you have a new dinner.

## Ingredients
### Base (all levels)
- Rice of choice (jasmine, basmati, long-grain), water at 1 : 1.25
- Neutral oil, salt, black pepper

### Level 1 — Burger Beef Bowl (1 serving)
- 250 g frozen lean ground beef (93/7)
- 10 g Kewpie mayo
- 10 g mustard
- 10 g ketchup
- Pinch smoked paprika
- Sweet-sour pickles, chopped
- Sesame seeds

### Level 2 — Gochujang Chicken Bowl
- Ground chicken
- 1 shallot, thinly sliced
- Thai bird's eye chilies, thinly sliced
- 10 g gochujang
- 10 g light soy sauce
- 5 g dark soy sauce
- 15 g rice vinegar
- Kewpie mayo (~10 g per bowl)
- Spring onions, finely sliced
- Pickled ginger

### Level 3 — Spiced Cumin Lamb Bowl
- Ground lamb
- Aleppo pepper
- Cumin seeds
- 1 spoon chicken stock powder, turmeric, a little dried oregano, 1–2 whole cloves (for the rice)
- Cherry tomatoes, Persian cucumbers, red onion, parsley
- Sumac, 10 g olive oil, 10 g vinegar
- Greek yogurt
- Feta

## Instructions
### Step 1: Freeze the meat (prep ahead)
1. Put the ground meat in a large freezer bag, press out the air and flatten it into the corners.
2. Score it through the bag into portions (e.g. 500 g → four 125 g pieces).
3. Freeze flat; break off only the portions you need.

### Step 2: Level 1 — Burger Beef Bowl
1. Start the rice in a rice cooker.
2. Stir mayo, mustard, ketchup and smoked paprika into a quick burger sauce.
3. Heat a pan very hot (200 °C+), add two frozen beef portions, season with salt and pepper.
4. Leave it alone until the edges shrink and the bottom crusts, then flip and break it up.
5. Bowl: rice, beef, all the sauce, a generous handful of pickles, sesame.

### Step 3: Level 2 — Gochujang Chicken Bowl
1. Mix gochujang, both soy sauces and rice vinegar into a stir-fry sauce.
2. Add oil and the chicken to a hot wok, salt it, press flat and leave ~3 minutes to brown.
3. Flip and break up; cook through.
4. Add a little more oil, the shallot and chilies; soften 1–2 minutes.
5. Pour in the sauce, let it sizzle 30 seconds, then toss until it reduces and coats the meat.
6. Bowl: rice, chicken, a squeeze of Kewpie mayo, spring onions, pickled ginger.

### Step 4: Level 3 — Spiced Cumin Lamb Bowl
1. Quarter the tomatoes, cut the cucumbers to the same size, slice the red onion into thin half-moons, chop the parsley.
2. Toss the salad with a pinch of salt, sumac, olive oil and vinegar; chill.
3. Put rice, stock powder, turmeric, oregano and cloves in the rice cooker with water at 1 : 1.25; cook.
4. Press the lamb into a very hot cast-iron pan; add ~1 % salt by weight, Aleppo pepper and cumin seeds.
5. Leave ~4 minutes to brown, then break up; blot off excess fat and fry 1–2 minutes more.
6. Bowl: yellow rice, lamb (reheat if made ahead), salad, a spoon of yogurt, crumbled feta.

## 💡 Notes & Tips
- **Macros (per bowl):** Level 1 ~688 kcal / 50 g protein · Level 2 ~760 kcal / 44 g · Level 3 ~623 kcal / 40 g.
- **Level 1 swaps:** any bottled sauce (buffalo, BBQ, salsa, hot sauce) and any pickle (onions — see [[rezepte/Pickled Onions\|Pickled Onions]] — jalapeños, peperoni).
- **Level 2 swaps:** beef or pork instead of chicken (pork and chicken are cheaper); garlic or ginger as aromatics; teriyaki, Thai-style or simply soy + sriracha as the sauce; basil, coriander or sesame as garnish. Leftover rice from Level 1 works perfectly.
- **Level 3 swaps:** coriander-lime, Mexican, dirty or coconut rice — just change what goes in the rice cooker. Za'atar, taco seasoning or a BBQ rub for the meat; slaw, roasted veg or pickles for the veg; sour cream, hummus or ranch as the creamy part.
- **Meal prep:** Level 3 components keep in the fridge; assemble and microwave at dinner.
- Pickled ginger is worth keeping in the fridge — it suits every level.
